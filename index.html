01 / MOLECULAR PHYSICSe⁻

How do proteins move electrons?

Think of a protein complex as a molecular relay: electrons hop between embedded sites. I model how distance, energy, and competing routes control where they go—and how light can drive charge separation.

The challenge: many interacting sites, different timescales, and a rapidly growing space of possible states. A “hole” is a missing electron; its movement is another way to describe charge transfer.

  • Electron-transfer theory
  • Protein design
  • Kinetic models
02 / MACHINE LEARNING + DE NOVO DESIGN↻

Design new molecules, then learn.

De novo means designing from the start. I combine chemical language models with reinforcement learning to propose small molecules and improve their predicted properties through repeated feedback.

For EGFR drug design, the reward balances predicted potency, drug-likeness, synthetic accessibility, and novelty. These are computational candidates—not experimentally validated treatments.

  • LLMs + reinforcement learning
  • De novo design
  • XGBoost + SHAP
03 / SCIENTIFIC AI AGENTS↗

AI that reasons—and calculates.

A scientific assistant should do more than explain. In Agentic MEKinetics, it can propose model changes, pass them through validation, and call a real physics engine to test a scientific question.

The challenge: connecting natural-language intent to explicit model parameters and reproducible numerical evidence. The demonstrated workflow is being evaluated more broadly.

  • Tool-using AI
  • Scientific validation
  • Reproducible results
04 / PARALLEL SCIENTIFIC COMPUTING∥

Explore more possibilities, together.

A single simulation is only the beginning. I distribute independent model runs across workers, use sparse numerical methods, and organize parameter searches to explore many possible molecular designs.

The challenge: controlling memory costs and coordinating batches while keeping every result tied to its model and settings. Physics provides the model; computation makes exploration practical.

  • Parallel algorithms
  • Sparse solvers
  • Slurm / HPC

01 / COMPUTATIONAL SCIENCEPublished · 2025–26

Using light to direct
charge through proteins.

Can a designed protein turn light into two distinct charge-transfer pathways? I model how electron vacancies—called “holes”—move through networks of molecular sites, splitting into branches with different energies. This process is called hole bifurcation.

Following our ACS Central Science paper, I combined kinetic modeling, XGBoost, SHAP, and Bayesian optimization to map 130,000 candidate energy landscapes and extract design rules for charge separation.

  • Kinetic modeling
  • XGBoost + SHAP
  • Bayesian optimization
  • DFT / MD / QM/MM

An AI assistant with a real physics engine.
