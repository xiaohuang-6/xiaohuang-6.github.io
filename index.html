Pre-aggregate logs and control cardinality

Consolidated action and attempt records into transaction metrics, then grouped them by client, caller, region, and fixed time windows. Stored these derived fingerprints in queryable aggregate tables, limiting high-cardinality grouping dimensions while retaining representative transactions for investigation. Split historical backfills into bounded time-range batches, ran batches in parallel, and used off-peak execution to manage shuffle-memory and I/O pressure.

Engineer interpretable behavioral signals

Worked with on-call engineers to define features such as waiting-time fraction: total idle time divided by total transaction duration within each window. Integrated and tuned an unsupervised Bayesian structural time-series model with seasonal baselines and pre- and post-event context. Evaluated abrupt spikes and gradual drift relative to each client’s history, validating sensitivity and false positives against human-reviewed examples.

Run model inference inside SQL

Integrated the model as a SQL table-valued function (TVF), keeping analysis alongside the aggregate data instead of exporting full datasets to Python. Precomputed aggregates support repeated investigations without rescanning all raw logs. Balanced aggregation granularity, query cost, and reliable batch completion against interactive latency and data freshness.

Separate the API from the dashboard

Built a Python JSON API that maps time-window, resolution, and filter selections to parameterized SQL query templates, then returns compact results to a modular TypeScript dashboard. Custom Canvas charts support grouping and drill-down from behavioral shifts to representative transaction context. The browser communicates through the API rather than querying the database directly.

Data freshness and engineering trade-offs

The deployed system used scheduled batch aggregation for retrospective diagnosis. The follow-on design targets a maximum data lag of approximately seven hours, making data from about seven hours ago and earlier available for investigation. This is a planned refresh capability, not real-time alerting. The tool supports human investigation rather than automatic incident remediation.

To limit model-evaluation cost, I designed a candidate-screening stage with Z-scores for abrupt spikes and Mann–Kendall tests for gradual trends, followed by Bayesian analysis. This is a proposed extension to the deployed SQL-native detector.

01 / PROTEIN DESIGN + INTERPRETABLE MLFirst author · 2025 & 2026

Designing proteins that
route light-generated charge.

Proteins move electrons through chains of molecular sites. I study how light-generated electron vacancies, or “holes,” can reach two destinations with different oxidizing strengths. The design challenge is to favor useful transfer routes while suppressing short circuits that waste the stored energy.

In our ACS Central Science study, we combined molecular dynamics, quantum-chemical calculations, kinetic master equations, and Bayesian optimization to study light-driven hole bifurcation. An illustrative model approached 100% simulated bifurcation yield.

The follow-up PCCP study examined 130,000 theoretical network configurations. An XGBoost classifier achieved 96.2% accuracy on 26,000 held-out simulated networks. SHAP analysis identified how site distances and electrostatic repulsion affect performance, including a trade-off between successful charge separation and energy efficiency.

What the model establishes

The network dataset varies physical distances and redox potentials; it is not a library of 130,000 protein sequences or atomistic structures. Atomistic calculations in the ACS study estimated about 1.6 eV of repulsion between the initially generated holes. The papers provide computational design rules, not experimental proof of a working light-powered protein.

02 / GENERATIVE AI + ANTICANCER DRUG DISCOVERYCorresponding author · 2026

Teaching a language model to design anticancer drug candidates.

I co-developed a ChemBERTa and reinforcement-learning pipeline to design small-molecule anticancer drug candidates targeting EGFR. Starting with 11,394 curated ChEMBL molecules, we adapted the chemical language model through masked-language training, then used a REINFORCE-style policy gradient to guide generation.

A multi-objective reward combines Random Forest-predicted potency, drug-likeness, synthetic accessibility, and novelty. RL increased mean composite reward from approximately 0.34 to 0.50, about 45% above the adapted baseline. A 1,000-molecule sample was fully valid under RDKit and contained no duplicates.

Evaluation and limits

Docking and nearest-neighbor comparisons assessed predicted binding and structural differences from known molecules. The improved reward came with a trade-off in predicted ease of synthesis. The potency model used a random train/test split, and the candidates have not been validated in biochemical or cellular experiments. The reported reward increase is not a measured increase in drug efficacy.

03 / ATOMISTIC MACHINE LEARNINGAccepted · awaiting publication

Testing AI force fields
for battery materials.

How reliably can an AI model predict atomic forces in a new environment? I co-developed a DFT-to-ML workflow for lithium on pristine, defective, and silicon-decorated graphene, building a 273-configuration DFT dataset and fine-tuning an equivariant MACE model.

On an independently defined test of 25 DFT configurations, fine-tuning reduced pooled force RMSE by 40.6%, from 450.0 to 267.5 meV/Å. Predictions improved across all five structural families and in 23 of the 25 configurations.

Transferability and my contribution

Structure-resolved tests exposed remaining force errors near highly strained carbon vacancies, showing where additional quantum-mechanical training data are needed. These results test local model transferability; they do not establish battery capacity, cycling performance, or diffusion coefficients. I am an equal-contribution and corresponding author, with shared methodology, software, analysis and writing, plus supervision.

Accepted by Computational Materials Science; awaiting formal publication. No public article link is available yet.

05 / SCIENTIFIC SOFTWARECo-author · 2022

AutoSolvate:
from solvent to simulation.

I co-authored AutoSolvate, an open-source Python toolkit for preparing solution-phase quantum-chemistry calculations. It automates solvent-box construction, force-field and charge preparation, molecular-dynamics sampling, and extraction of molecular clusters through Python, command-line, and graphical interfaces.

The paper demonstrated the workflow on 166 redox couples, obtaining converged reorganization-energy results for 151 systems. A model trained on simulation-generated data predicted solvent-solute spacing in acetonitrile (MeCN) with a 0.09 Å mean absolute error on 83 held-out systems.

This project connects reproducible simulation setup with data generation for machine learning in chemistry.
